# Living Archive Entry Builder

Use this skill to preserve the path around a thing, not only the thing itself.

The archive should feel quiet, structured, personal without being confessional, and practical rather than purely philosophical.

## Who This Is For

Use this for builders, researchers, artists, founders, students, or reflective people who want their work to preserve life context over time. It should be installable as a general skill, not dependent on one person's archive.

## Trigger Phrases

- "Turn this into an archive entry."
- "Preserve this project."
- "Write the context around this thing."
- "What changed because of this?"
- "Make this into a living archive note."

## Inputs

Accept:

- Project notes
- Git history summaries
- Journal entries
- Screenshots or product pages
- Conversations about why something mattered
- A messy description of a life period or realization

Read existing archive conventions before editing archive content. Preserve flexible narrative blocks instead of forcing a rigid form.

## Entry Shape

A good entry answers:

- What was made or realized?
- What period of life did it belong to?
- What pressure, desire, or confusion created it?
- What changed because of it?
- What should future self not forget?
- What thread does it connect to?

Keep the philosophy implicit. Let continuity show through concrete details.

## Voice

Use:

- specific nouns
- short paragraphs
- concrete transitions
- enough context to be understood later

Avoid:

- generic founder/portfolio language
- over-explaining the worldview
- making every project sound like a formal case study
- flattening personal notes into productivity content

## Output

Return either a draft entry or patch existing archive content.

For a draft:

```text
Title:
Time period:
Thread:
Pull:
Blocks:
- paragraph:
- list:
- paragraph:
Future-self note:
```

For an edit, keep the existing content model and add only what improves continuity.
